# Quality Systems (QA / QC)

Quality Assurance (QA) and Quality Control (QC) built on **Human & Organizational Performance (HOP)**.

**Topics:** QA, QC, HOP, systems-based thinking, operational learning, better investigations

## Why HOP for quality

Traditional quality systems often treat deviations, batch losses, and recurring defects as "human error" and respond with retraining, discipline, or another signature on the procedure. HOP is a risk-based operating philosophy that recognizes error is part of the human condition. It holds that processes, systems, and culture shape the decisions and actions of frontline workers, so the system is where quality is won or lost. This domain applies that view to QA and QC, following the approach in *Do Quality Differently* by Clifford Berry and Amy Wilson ([doqualitydifferently.com](https://doqualitydifferently.com/)).

### The five HOP principles

1. **People make mistakes.** Error is normal. Design the system to expect it, catch it, and recover from it.
2. **Blame fixes nothing.** Blame ends learning. Find out why the action made sense at the time.
3. **Context drives behavior.** Conditions, pressures, tools, and guidance shape what people do.
4. **Learning and improving is vital.** Learn from everyday work as well as from failures.
5. **How we react matters.** Leaders' responses to bad news decide whether bad news keeps coming.

### QA and QC through a HOP lens

| Traditional practice | HOP-informed practice |
| --- | --- |
| Root cause is "operator error" and the fix is retraining | Investigate the conditions that made the error likely, then fix the system |
| Procedures written for auditors | Human-focused written guidance written for the people doing the work |
| Quality measured only by deviations and failures (Safety-I) | Also learn from what goes right, where people adapt to make work succeed (Safety-II) |
| Inspection as the main defense | Layered defenses, error-tolerant design, and capacity to fail safely |
| One response for every event | Risk-based response triggers: respond in proportion to the potential consequence |
| Accountability means consequences for individuals | Real accountability means owning the learning and improving the system |
| Work as imagined | Work as done, understood through Gemba and learning teams |
